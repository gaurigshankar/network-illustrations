2. FIGURES THAT ARE ILLUSTRATIVE RATHER THAN AUTHORITATIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EVERY numeric figure in this video is a representative range for typical LTE
macro equipment. None is a manufacturer specification. This is stated on
screen as a persistent caveat from cue 5.9 onward, and individual values carry
"(typical)" or "representative" labels at the point of use.

The figures are drawn from the content models already in this repository
(README.md, prompts/cabinet-interior.md, prompts/signal-chain.md), not
invented for the video. They are still illustrative:

  Tower and RF
    tower height 30-60 m; antenna 1.4-2.6 m, 15-18 dBi, 65 deg beamwidth
    mechanical downtilt 0-10 deg
    coax feeder loss ~5 dB/100 m at 1800 MHz; fibre ~0.35 dB/km
    RRU mass 10-25 kg; 40-60 W idle, 300-700 W loaded
    RRU output 40-46 dBm (~10-40 W per branch)
    handset transmit <= 23 dBm (~0.2 W)
    RRU receive sensitivity -100 to -110 dBm
    path loss 120-145 dB

  Digital
    CPRI line rates 2.5 / 4.9 / 9.8 / 10.1 Gbps
    backhaul 1-10 Gbps
    sample rate ~15.36 Msps for a 10 MHz carrier
    scheduling granularity 1 ms TTI
    IP MTU ~1500 B

  Power plant
    bus float ~54 V; low-voltage disconnect ~43 V; the sag curve between them
      is a teaching illustration, not a measured discharge profile
    4 x 12 V VRLA, 100-200 Ah, 2-8 h autonomy
    breaker schedule (F1-F3 32 A, F4 20 A, F5 10 A, B1 125 A) is illustrative
    cabinet ~2.0 m x ~0.8 m, 42 U rail

  Scale strip (scene 5)
    the four plotted points — ~10 Gbps, ~1.8 GHz, ~40 W, ~1 pW — are
    order-of-magnitude markers chosen to show the span. The "fifteen orders of
    magnitude" claim refers to the power span between what leaves the radio and
    what reaches the handset, not to the strip's full plotted axis.

  Not modelled at all
    the discharge curve's shape, alarm escalation timing, and the exact
    thresholds at which a given vendor's controller raises each alarm.

3. BEATS CUT OR RE-STAGED BECAUSE THEY DEPENDED ON INTERACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The source material in docs/ is a set of click-to-drill-down D3 diagrams.
Video has no clicks, so every interaction became a timed, narrated beat.

RE-STAGED

  Click-to-expand callouts (all three diagrams)
    -> reveals keyed to the narration line that names them. Scene 3's seven RRU
       parts and scene 4's four shelves now fire in narration order rather than
       in whatever order a user clicked.

  Hover tooltips
    -> on-screen labels that fade in as they are spoken. In scene 5 this became
       the detail strip under the chain, which shows the active node's figures
       while that node is the subject of the sentence.

  Drill-down levels (cabinet overview -> shelf -> module)
    -> camera pushes. Scene 3 opens with a scale-and-translate push into the
       tower-top region that resolves into the RRU three-quarter view; scene 4
       pulls back down to the cabinet.

  Layer toggles (physical / power / signal / thermal / earthing)
    -> cross-fades on a narration cue. Scene 3's feeder-loss comparison and
       scene 4's stacking-rationale panel both arrive this way.

  Mains-failure simulation (scrub and step controls)
    -> a fixed 25-second animation. The bus-voltage readout counts down on the
       timeline instead of following a scrub handle, and each stage names the
       alarm a technician would actually see.

  Fault-localisation symptom picker
    -> scene 6's fixed sequence, one symptom per narration line, with the
       suspect stages pulsing.

  RRU LED fault state (a hover/inspect state in the source)
    -> a live green-to-red event at cue 3.12, with a burned-in "RUN: FAULT"
       text label. Status is never conveyed by colour alone anywhere in the
       video.

  Breaker schedule table (click a row to highlight a breaker, and vice versa)
    -> reduced to labelled breakers drawn on the DC panel (F1-F5, B1). The
       cross-highlighting was the whole point of the table, so the table itself
       was cut rather than shown as a static block the viewer cannot query.

CUT

  IQ sub-visual with selectable modulation order (QPSK / 16-QAM / 64-QAM)
    The source expands this inline when the IQ stage is selected. Selecting a
    modulation order is the teaching mechanism; without it the constellation is
    decoration. IQ is named and defined in narration ("numbers that describe a
    waveform") and appears as a chain stage, but the sub-visual is not drawn.

  Latency budget layer
    A proportional latency breakdown needs comparison against a baseline the
    viewer chooses. It did not survive as a fixed 5-second beat and would have
    pushed scene 5 past its budget.

  Physical-mapping layer (chain stages tied by leader lines to a site
  silhouette)
    Superseded by the persistent locator graphic, which does the same job
    continuously rather than as a toggled layer.

  "Dropped call" symptom
    The source lists five symptoms; scene 6 has room for four in 30 seconds.
    Dropped call overlaps heavily with high uplink noise and slow throughput in
    its first on-site check, so it was the one to drop.

  Keyboard navigation, ARIA labelling, breadcrumbs, reduced-motion handling
    Not applicable to a linear render. The accessibility intent that does carry
    over is honoured: text contrast passes the checker at every sampled frame,
    status always carries a text label, and captions are burned in throughout.

4. PRESENTER STAGING (OPTION B) AND WHAT IS NOT IN THIS RENDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRESENTER — cold open and recap only

  The presenter appears FULL FRAME in scene 1 and scene 7, and nowhere else.
  Scenes 2-6 carry no presenter, so the diagrams own the whole frame.

  This replaced an earlier staging in which a corner cutout rode along at ~22%
  frame width for the whole video. That cutout held a permanent keep-out of
  x 40-462, y 473-1004 - about a fifth of the frame, reserved and empty - and
  scenes 3, 5 and 6 had all been squeezed rightward to clear it. Removing it
  let the feeder-loss comparison, the signal-chain detail strip, the
  logarithmic scale strip, the uplink row and the symptom cards all reclaim
  that space; they now start at x 140 instead of x 520.

  It also cut the avatar footage needed from 340 s to 46 s.

  TO DROP THE TALKING HEAD IN
    Two clips are required, at these exact durations:
      assets/presenter-open.mp4    17.61 s   covers cues 1.1 - 1.3
      assets/presenter-close.mp4   28.48 s   covers cues 7.1 - 7.6
    Both are staged at x 140, y 140, 620 x 780 in index.html. Replace the
    placeholder contents of #avatar-frame-open / #avatar-frame-close with
      <video src="assets/presenter-open.mp4" muted></video>
    and re-render. No other change is needed; the geometry and the clip
    windows are already correct. The spec lives in content/content.js under
    `avatar.clips`.

  The web UI export is sufficient - the HeyGen API is not required for this.

NOT IN THIS RENDER

  Avatar / talking head
    Not generated. This environment has no HeyGen credential, so
    POST /v3/videos could not run. The slots above are staged and waiting.

  Narration audio
    HeyGen TTS is behind the same credential. Free local neural TTS is also
    unavailable here: piper-tts and kokoro-onnx install from PyPI, but their
    voice models are hosted on huggingface.co, which the environment's egress
    proxy refuses (403 on CONNECT, confirmed). The same wall blocks the
    open-source lip-sync models (SadTalker, Wav2Lip, MuseTalk), whose weights
    live on HuggingFace or GitHub releases. espeak-ng would install and run
    offline, but its formant synthesis is not fit for training material.

    The narration is therefore carried by the burned-in captions and the .srt.
    All scene timing is built around the spoken word count at 145 wpm, so
    attaching real voiceover later will not require retiming.

  Music bed and SFX
    No media-generation access; a synthesised pad would have been worse than
    silence, so the render carries no audio track. The mains-failure sequence
    already has the duck window reserved in its timing.

  Composited cloud render
    POST /v3/hyperframes/renders needs the same credential. Rendered locally
    instead, with Chromium plus FFmpeg 6.1.1.
